azsb – Azure Service Bus CLI and terminal UI

Browse Azure Service Bus namespaces, queues, topics and subscriptions, peek and receive messages, and resend or retry dead-letter messages, all from your terminal.

What it does

  • Lists the Azure subscriptions of your az login and their Service Bus namespaces; no connection strings
  • Queues, topics and subscriptions with live active, dead-letter, scheduled and transfer counts
  • Peek, receive (PeekLock) or receive-and-delete messages; complete, abandon or dead-letter locked ones
  • Retry dead-letter messages to their queue or topic, in transactions on Standard and Premium namespaces
  • Resend copies of messages, purge queues, delete entities
  • Azure Monitor charts of throughput and backlog, from 1 hour to 30 days
  • Works with the local Service Bus emulator
  • Native executables for Linux, macOS and Windows (x64 and arm64)

A Service Bus Explorer alternative for the terminal

The Azure portal's Service Bus Explorer and the standalone Service Bus Explorer app need a browser or Windows. azsb does the same day-to-day work (finding the queue with dead letters, reading why messages failed, sending them back) from any terminal, over SSH too, with the keyboard or the mouse.

Questions

How do I browse Azure Service Bus messages from the command line?

Install azsb, run az login, then azsb. Pick a namespace, open a queue or subscription and peek its active or dead-letter messages. azsb -s "My subscription" -n my-namespace -o orders/dlq opens a dead-letter queue directly.

How do I retry dead-letter messages in Azure Service Bus?

Open the queue or subscription, switch to the dead-letter tab, select the messages and press Shift+R. On Standard and Premium namespaces each batch moves in one transaction.

Does azsb need a connection string?

No. It signs in with your Azure CLI login and also connects to the local Service Bus emulator.
